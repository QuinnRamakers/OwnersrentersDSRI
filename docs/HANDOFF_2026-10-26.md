# Handoff, 26 October 2026: production numerics at the absorbing floor of EUR 5,000

For claude.ai chat and the next Claude Code session. It covers the work of
24-26 October, after `docs/HANDOFF_2026-10-24.md`. The detailed numbers are in
the `HANDOFF.md` log entries of 24 and 25 October; this file says what was
decided, what was found, what is open, and how to reproduce it.

All runs are ladder step 16 (the calibration before step 17 below) with the
absorbing floor at c_min = EUR 5,000 and the exact bankruptcy test on. Owners
charge maintenance on the current house value unless the purchase price is
named.

## 2. What was decided

1. Search routine (24 October): the sweep, then fmincon active-set, with the
   sweep's second round starting at the first round's half-step
   (`fmincon_algorithm = 'active-set'`, `N_c = 11`). Interior-point stays an
   option and is the robustness check. In `config.params` since 448c0a3.
2. Edge fix rejected (25 October): filling the grid points below the floor
   from the solvent side (`abs_fill`, branch edge-fix, not merged) made the
   small grids worse, not better.
3. Grid (25 October): u1 at 32, u2 at 16, u3 at 40, q at 4 base points, bunched
   u1 and u2 (`grid_pow = 3`, `grid_pow_u2 = 3`), the limit share where
   nothing is saved, 5 quadrature nodes. Not yet in `config.params`; the u3
   spacing is open (section 5).
4. Maintenance (26 October, for now): owners pay maintenance on the purchase
   price. Appended as ladder step 17 (`maint_purchase`); steps 7-16 keep the
   current value, set at step 1. `config.params` has `maint_on_purchase = true`.

## 3. What was found

### 3.1 The search routine

1. The sweep before fmincon is needed. Without it, 1-10% of grid points settle
   on the wrong choice, and the households' mean equity share moves by about
   0.04 at 66 and 90. At step 13 (no floor, maintenance on the purchase price)
   dropping it changed little except the young owners' equity share, in half
   the time.
2. sqp settles for a worse choice at up to a third of the grid points at 66.
3. Active-set with the wider sweep gives the same life cycles as
   interior-point (consumption within 0.05%, equity share within 0.012
   through 95) and solves in about 60% of the time (owner 24x24: 27 against
   44 minutes; renter 32x32: 16 against 28).

### 3.2 Each grid axis on its own

From the base of bunched 16x16, 10 u3 points and (owners) q at 4:

| Axis | Renter | Owner, current value |
|---|---|---|
| u1, income over total resources | lowers consumption at 30 by 9% (32) and 12% (64); still moving at 64 | lowers consumption at 30 by 8% (32) and 11% (64); still moving at 64 |
| u2, pension and housing over non-income wealth | no change at 32 or 64 | no change at 32 or 64 |
| u3, pension against house or rent index | raises peak saving 10%, 17%, 20% at 20, 40, 80 points; bankrupt by 85 from 6.4% to 2.5% | raises peak saving 33%, 60%, 69%; bankrupt by 85 from 14.3% to 7.8%; retirement equity share from 0.85 to 0.6-0.75 |
| q, purchase price over house value | none | no change at 8 or 16 |
| quadrature, 5 against 7 nodes | no change | no change |
| all doubled at once | a little more than the single doublings added up | a little more than the single doublings added up |

So u1 and u2, which the earlier studies always refined together, behave
differently: u1 matters and u2 does not. u3 matters most and settles between
40 and 80 points. Renters do best with u3 evenly spaced (40 even points are
within 1% of 80 bunched); owners with u3 bunched toward 0 (old owners sit at
u3 0.04-0.2). Nothing settles before about 35-40, and the entry value (the
welfare level) keeps falling with every refinement.

The axes converge slowly: each doubling of u3 cuts the change only 1.6-1.8
times. The cause is not settled. Interpolating across the edge of bankruptcy
was the suspect; the fill that removes it on the solvent side did not help
(section 2).

### 3.3 The confirming grids

1. Renter, u1 64, u2 16, u3 40 evenly spaced (66x18x40, 68 minutes): its life
   cycles lie on top of the 24 October candidate (32x32, u3 40 even), a
   different grid of the same size, from about 40 on; only young consumption
   differs (4% at 30). Bankrupt by 85: 1.7%.
2. Owner, u1 32, u2 16, u3 40 bunched, q 4 (34x18x40x5, 100 minutes): matches
   u3 at 80 in retirement and adds the u1 effect in working life. Bankrupt by
   85: 6.7%, the lowest of all owner runs. It was not checked against a grid
   finer in both u1 and u3 at once.

### 3.4 The maintenance rule

On the purchase price (same five owner grids):

1. The grid barely matters: consumption agrees within about 2% at every age;
   u3 makes no visible difference, u1 at 32 lowers saving around 60 a little,
   q at 8 moves bankruptcy by 0.2 points.
2. Bankruptcy is about 1%, all while the mortgage runs (none after 54), against
   7-14% by 85 on the current value.
3. Owners save about a fifth as much (peak near 1 times entry income against
   3-5), and hold almost all their liquid saving in equity in retirement.
4. Solves take 2-2.5 times as long, since q enters the budget at every age.

The large u3 effect on the current value comes from old-age bankruptcy, which
the purchase-price rule removes. The chosen grid is safe under both rules; on
the purchase price its u3 points cost time without changing the answer.

### 3.5 The production numerics as they were

Uniform 20x20, u3 at 12, q at 8, pi = 1 where nothing is saved: owners 6-14%
off in consumption at every age and up to 0.47 in the equity share; renters
3-4% off at 40-66. They spend points on u2 and q, which need none, and too few
on u1 and u3.

## 5. Decisions open, in order

1. Put the chosen grid into `config.params` and the ladder's numerics, and how
   to space u3 with one setting for both tenures (evenly spaced suits renters;
   on the purchase price owners hardly need u3, so evenly spaced is enough).
2. Adopt the absorbing floor in production, and the value of c_min (production
   still has c_min 0).
3. Whether the owner grid needs a check finer in both u1 and u3 (u1 64 with
   u3 40, about 3.5 hours on the current value, longer on the purchase price).
4. Why the grid axes converge slowly (section 3.2).
5. The maintenance rule beyond "for now": the purchase price removes old-age
   bankruptcy that the current value produces; equity release (TODO.md, item
   1) is the other route.

## 6. How to reproduce, in order

From the repository root in MATLAB:

1. `addpath diagnostics/nibud diagnostics/pension_grid`.
2. Solve with `solve_cases` (`<step>:<arm>:<case>`), two processes at a time.
   Case names: `pow3_<N1>x<N2>_lim_u3p2|u3even[_u3n<n>][_qn<n>]_cmin5000_as_nc11[_mop]`;
   for example `16:owner:pow3_32x16_lim_u3p2_qn4_u3n40_cmin5000_as_nc11`. Cases
   solved before 448c0a3 without a routine suffix used interior-point and N_c
   41 (reproduce with `_ip_nc41`).
3. The pages: `axis_study_page('renter')`, `axis_study_page('owner')`,
   `confirm_grid_page`, `maintenance_rule_page`, `production_lifecycles`,
   `production_tenure_page('renter')`, and for the search routine
   `optimiser_step` / `optimiser_figure` and `sweep_lifecycles`.

Trap: `case_params` builds on `ladder.params(16)`, which keeps maintenance on
the current value; add `_mop` for the purchase price.
