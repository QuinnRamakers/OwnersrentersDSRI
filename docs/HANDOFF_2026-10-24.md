## 2. What was built

The model. A household whose liquid resources fall below the floor
F_t = phi_t Y_t goes bankrupt. It consumes F at that level every year until it
dies and makes no further choices; its liquid wealth, pension pot, house and
later income are gone. Its value is u(F) D_t, with D_t = 1 + beta p_t D_{t+1}
and D_T = 1. The floor is also the lowest consumption a solvent household can
choose. No state variable is added: the floor is proportional to income, so the
value stays homothetic.

Quinn's decisions (23 October):

1. The floor is absorbing for life, fixed at its level in the year of
   bankruptcy, linked to labour income, with no new state.
2. The share by age: phi_t = c_min / Ybar_t, so the median household's floor is
   c_min euros at every age; in retirement it is tied to final labour income.
3. One share for both tenures, set on the owners' income profile until the
   pooled income cubic is in. A median renter's floor is then 0.757 c_min.
4. The floor is the search's lower bound; bankruptcy is involuntary.
5. The exact bankruptcy test at the quadrature nodes is on by default (24
   October).
6. The plots are not weighted by saving.

The code:

1. `+config/params.m`: `c_min` (0, off), `phi_abs` (the alternative, one share
   of permanent labour income; 0, off), `abs_node_test` (true).
2. `+config/derive.m`: `phi_path`, `D_abs`, `floor_absorbing`; refuses both
   floors at once, `c_min` with income not in euros, negative values and a
   floor not above `phi_floor`.
3. `+solver/bellman_step_lna.m`: the floor from `phi_path(t)`; bankrupt nodes
   valued at the closed form, with no continuation or bequest. With
   `abs_node_test`, next year's liquid resources are formed at every quadrature
   node and the bankrupt value is used there exactly, instead of interpolating
   across the jump in the value at the edge of bankruptcy.
4. `+simulate/paths_lna.m`: `absorbed` and `abs_age`; consumption fixed at
   entry, wealth zero after, no bequest, no policy lookup, not counted off the
   grid. Results saved before the change still re-simulate.
5. `+model/summarize.m`, `checks.m`, `print_checks.m`, `floor_kind.m`: wealth,
   housing and shares over solvent households; `abs_hazard`; the bankrupt share
   at 40, 67 and 100 and entry rates by five-year band; "bankrupt" in place of
   "floored" in the figures and the ladder tables.
6. Diagnostics: `diagnostics/nibud/absorbing_economics.m`, `absorbing_income.m`,
   `absorbing_grid.m`; `case_params` suffixes `_cmin<n>`, `_exact`, `_interp`;
   `policy_gaps`, `ruin_interp`, `case_load` read bankrupt households correctly.
7. Tests: `floor_derive`, `floor_off_identical` (step 16, both tenures, small
   grid, floor off, bit-identical to 7b5143d), `floor_absorbing` (closed form at
   bankrupt nodes, panel invariants, dashboard), `floor_node_test`. The full
   suite: 0 of 21 failed.

## 3. What was found

### 3.1 Behaviour

1. Young households stop starving themselves. Median consumption at 25, in
   multiples of entry income, rises from 0.03 to 0.23-0.30 for owners with
   maintenance on the current value and from 0.17 to 0.27-0.38 for renters. With
   ruin bounded there is no motive to save everything against it.
2. They save less and hold equities. Liquid saving at 40 falls from 3.8 to
   0.6-1.2 times entry income (owners, current value) and from 2.9 to 1.1-2.2
   (renters). The equity share of liquid saving at 30-55 rises from 0.01-0.03
   (owners) to 0.1-0.9.
3. Higher floors bring more bankruptcy, less saving and a higher entry value
   (except the owner on the purchase price at 12,000: 0.328 against 0.343).
4. c_min 12,000 is too high: the floor (0.30 of income at 25) sits just under
   the owners' cash after housing at entry (0.33), and 54-58% of owners are
   bankrupt by 30.

### 3.2 Bankruptcy

Cumulative % bankrupt at 40 / 67 / 100, of the households alive at that age
(the panel has no deaths); for the top-up, % ever floored.

| | top-up | 4,000 | 6,000 | 8,000 | 10,000 | 12,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owner, current value | 0 / 0.2 / 37 | 0.2 / 3.3 / 47 | 0.7 / 5.3 / 51 | 2.2 / 8.7 / 55 | 6.2 / 14 / 60 | 59 / 62 / 82 |
| Owner, purchase price | 0 / 0.4 / 0.4 | 0.1 / 0.9 / 0.9 | 0.2 / 1.6 / 1.6 | 1.3 / 3.0 / 3.0 | 4.0 / 6.3 / 6.3 | 54 / 55 / 55 |
| Renter | 0 / 0.1 / 7.4 | 0 / 0.7 / 15 | 0 / 1.5 / 19 | 0.1 / 2.7 / 24 | 0.3 / 4.6 / 32 | 1.6 / 8.4 / 41 |

1. Owners with maintenance on the current value go bankrupt in old age: 1.5% of
   a house whose real price grows 3.7% a year outruns the first pillar.
2. Owners with maintenance on the purchase price go bankrupt only while the
   mortgage runs (to 54).
3. Retired renters run their savings down into the floor; one-year rates of
   1-1.5% after 85.

### 3.3 Owner bankruptcy is illiquidity, not insolvency

At c_min 8,000, 99.9% of the owners on the current value who go bankrupt still
hold positive home equity in the year they do: a median of 50 times entry
income, at a median age of 88. On the purchase price, 96% hold equity (median
5.3 times entry income, median age 45). The model lets no one sell the house
or borrow against it, so a bankrupt owner gives up a house worth decades of
income for the floor. Equity release (TODO.md, item 1.1, and the 21 October
"sold, renting for life" proposal) is what would change this.

### 3.4 Net income after housing costs

1. Net income after housing depends on no choice, so it is the same with and
   without the floor (checked in every solvent year).
2. At 25 every household has 1-2 times its floor. Below its own floor at 100:
   about half of the owners on the current value (most of them below zero),
   none on the purchase price after 54, about a quarter of the renters. The
   bankrupt share follows the share below the floor.
3. In the year of bankruptcy every household's net income is below its floor
   (median 0.63 of it for owners on the current value, 0.77 on the purchase
   price, 0.76 for renters; below zero for 16%, 1% and 15%). Before that they
   run their liquid wealth down over about ten years.
4. Renters who go bankrupt have low incomes all their life, so their floor in
   euros is low too (median about EUR 3,700): the price of a floor
   proportional to own income.

### 3.5 Numerical behaviour

1. The ruin corner is priced correctly. The interpolated certainty equivalent
   next to ruin was off by up to 1e5-1e7% with the top-up; with the floor the
   median gap is under 1% everywhere, and the largest is 86-108% at 90 in a few
   per cent of the corner (owner 24x24, with the exact test).
2. Consumption is stable across grids. With the top-up, owner grids disagreed
   by 70-160,000% before 55 and renter grids by 1,450-10,000% before 40. With
   the floor, owner 24x24 against 32x32 and renter
   32x32 against 48x48 are within 2% from 40 on; the summary grids within 2-8%.
   The owners' hoarding with 20 points on u3 is gone (5-8% gaps remain on that
   axis).
3. The equity share does not converge on any grid, with or without the floor.
   The grid test's stop rule (2% in consumption, 0.02 in the share from 25 on)
   is not met.
4. The entry value (0.26-0.29 of entry income, against 0.000-0.022 with the
   top-up) falls 4-5% with each refinement: owner 0.285, 0.271, 0.261 at 16x16,
   24x24 and 32x32. Welfare levels are not converged.
5. The exact bankruptcy test (`abs_node_test`) changed consumption, bankruptcy
   and the entry value by under half a point, roughly halved the old-age
   equity-share gaps between grids, and cut the largest ruin-corner error by a
   fifth. The entry value's drift is therefore not from the edge of
   bankruptcy; the remaining error sits on the solvent side, along u3 for old
   owners with almost no pot.
6. The solver stores c = 1 and pi = 1 at bankrupt nodes, and solvent households
   next to them read interpolated policies. Replacing those values by the
   nearest solvent node moves bankruptcy, consumption and saving by at most a
   point on the coarse owner grid and not at all on the fine ones, but lowers
   the plotted equity share at 95 from about 1.0 to 0.88 (owner 32x32). Those
   households save almost nothing. Left as it is (Quinn).

### 3.6 Bugs looked for

None found. The checks: the floor off is bit-identical to the old code; the
value at bankrupt nodes equals the closed form to 1e-12; the owners' bankrupt
nodes match an independently written budget (0 mismatches over 80,006 and
93,556 node-years); the panel's bankrupt households stay bankrupt at a constant
consumption with no wealth or bequest; net income agrees between the panels in
every solvent year; bankruptcy rises and saving falls monotonically in c_min;
off-grid lookups are 0 in every solve.

### 3.7 Run time

The exact test costs 4-7% (one process, idle machine, step 16, c_min 8,000,
gh_n 5: renter 214 s against 228 s, owner 339 s against 351 s). The solve
times printed in the grid-set entries are not comparable with each other: those
runs shared the machine with up to three others.

## 4. Decisions open, in order

1. Adopt the floor (append ladder step 17 with `c_min` and set `config.params`
   to match), or first give owners a way out of the house (equity release),
   since that is what drives owner bankruptcy.
2. The value and source of `c_min`: the Nibud minimum example budgets, spending
   after housing at the bijstand level, which also settles single or couple.
   8,000 is the working value; 12,000 is too high.
3. A bequest rule for bankrupt households before `chi` is switched on (a
   household that leaves nothing has a bequest utility of minus infinity).
4. When the pooled income cubic is in, the floor's share comes from it for both
   tenures (the code takes the owners' profile).
5. The u3 axis for old owners with a small pot, which carries the remaining
   ruin-corner error and probably the entry value's drift.
6. The equity share's convergence (it predates the floor).

## 5. What the results can be quoted for

1. Behaviour with the floor from 40 on: consumption on owner 24x24 or renter
   32x32 and finer; the summary grids within 2-8%.
2. Bankruptcy rates and their pattern by age and floor level, with the caveat
   of section 3.3 for owners. They move by 1-2 points across the u1 x u2 grids,
   more with u3 at 20 points (owners bankrupt at 95: 45% against 39%).
3. Not the equity share, and no welfare levels.
4. The model at 12,000 only as a limit case.

## 6. How to reproduce, in order

From the repo root in MATLAB:

1. `addpath diagnostics/nibud diagnostics/pension_grid; setenv('CGM_OUTPUT_DIR', pwd)`.
2. Solve with `solve_cases`. The summary grids: owner
   `16:owner:pow3_16x16_lim_u3p2_qn4_nosweep_cmin<n>` and the same with
   `_mop` at the end, renter `16:renter:pow3_16x16_lim_u3p2_u3n20_cmin<n>`,
   for n = 4000, 6000, 8000, 10000, 12000. The grid set at 8,000: owner
   `pow3_24x24_lim_u3p2_qn4`, `pow3_32x32_lim_u3p2`,
   `pow3_24x24_lim_u3p2_qn4_u3n20`; renter `pow3_32x32_lim_u3p2`,
   `pow3_48x48_lim_u3p2`, `pow3_16x16_lim_u3p2_u3n40`; each with
   `_cmin8000_exact`. Run two `matlab -batch` processes at a time, smallest
   grids first. The top-up cases of 18-20 October (the same names without
   `_cmin`) must be there too.
3. `absorbing_economics` (the economics page, dashboards, overlays, tables).
4. `absorbing_income` (the two net-income figures).
5. `absorbing_grid(8000, extra = '_exact')` (the grid pages and gaps).
6. `ruin_interp(16, 'owner', {'pow3_24x24_lim_u3p2_qn4', 'pow3_24x24_lim_u3p2_qn4_cmin8000_exact'})`.
7. `addpath tests; setenv('CGM_FLOOR_BASELINE', <the baseline file>); run_tests`.
   The baseline (step 16, both tenures, small grid, solved at 7b5143d) is
   `C:\Users\Quinn\Desktop\claudecodetest\floor_baseline_7b5143d.mat`, outside
   the repo; without it `floor_off_identical` reports SKIP.

Two traps:

1. The `_cmin<n>` solves of 23 October without `_exact` were made with the
   exact test off. The same names now build it on, so `solve_cases` would
   solve them again under the same file names. To reproduce them, add
   `_interp`.
2. `CGM_STATE_GRID` and `CGM_GH_N` reach only `ladder.numerics` (so
   `ladder.run`) and `run_model`, not `ladder.params` or `config.derive`. For a
   small grid set `p.grid_dims` and `p.gh_n` before deriving.
