## 2. What goes wrong at step 7

1. **The mechanism.** The owner pays a housing bill each year of k times the
   current house value. k is 1.5% maintenance plus the mortgage annuity rate
   (4.08%) up to age 54, and 1.5% after. The house cannot be sold or borrowed
   against. If savings plus income do not cover the bill, the household
   consumes the floor, 1e-6 times income. With gamma = 5, one floored year
   weighs as much as 1e24 normal years, so any reachable bankruptcy dominates
   the household's choices.
2. **Where bankruptcy starts.** With no savings, at a house above 1/k years of
   household income: 17.9 before 55, 66.7 after. With savings x it is
   (1 + x)/k. The house grows 3.73% a year in real terms while the pension is
   flat, so retired owners drift towards the edge.
3. **The model's mortgage.** It charges the annuity rate on the current house
   value, not on the loan. In present value it costs 1.71 times the loan
   (median 1.64), against 1.00 for a fixed-rate annuity (`figures.mortgage`).
4. **Why the grid mattered.** u1 (income over total wealth) was spaced evenly
   on [0.0002, 0.6]. In wealth over income the points jump 11.5, 23.9, 4,999.
   The solver interpolates linearly in z, roughly well-being times u1, between
   those points. That hides bankruptcy in old age: households at the floor are
   valued at 0.25-0.65 years of income. It also misses the steep fall in young
   owners' value between wealth of about 4 and 25 years of income.

The grids:

1. **current**: u1 evenly spaced on [0.0002, 0.6]; u2 (house over savings plus
   house) evenly spaced on [0, 1]. The entry anchors are added as exact points.
2. **log-wealth**: u1 evenly spaced in log(1 + wealth/income), three times
   denser between 2 and 60 years of income. Half the u2 points are evenly
   spaced on [0, 0.9]; the other half are evenly spaced in log(savings/house)
   from 0.11 down to 0.0005; then u2 = 1.
3. **edge-aligned**: log-wealth, with points moved onto the bankruptcy edge
   s = k - u1 (1 + k). This was Quinn's suggestion.
4. **power 2 / power 3**: the existing primitives `grid_pow` and `grid_pow_u2`
   at 2 or 3. u1 is bunched towards high wealth and u2 towards "everything in
   the house".

What the study shows:

1. The current placement does not converge. At 64x48 it still has 2.3% of
   household-years bankrupt, against 0.35%.
2. Log-wealth and power 2 converge to the same answer from different
   placements. At 64x48 both are close to 128x96 in the bankruptcy rate by age
   (figure 1), the value function along the no-savings line (figure 2) and the
   equity share. Power 2 at 64x48 is the closest of all. The remaining gap is
   consumption at 80-95 and savings around 80.
3. At 32x32 the power grids still under-resolve old-age bankruptcy (2.4-3.8%);
   log-wealth does not (0.59%). Aligning points with the edge adds nothing,
   because the value has already collapsed well before the edge.
4. Runtime: `solve_sec` is the full backward induction over 76 ages for one
   tenure, without the simulation. It costs about 0.1 s per state-grid point
   (u1 x u2 x 2), whatever the placement. That is about 1.3 ms per point per
   age.
5. **The renter** (same grids, gaps to renter log-wealth 128x96):
   1. Renters almost never go bankrupt (0.36% of household-years on the
      current grid, about 0 on the others).
   2. From about 40 on every grid agrees: consumption within a few percent,
      the equity share from about 55.
   3. Young renters do not converge on any grid. The share of renters aged
      25-39 at the consumption bound rises with every refinement: 0% at
      16x16, 11-32% at 32x32, 29-40% at 64x48 and 78% at 128x96. The largest
      gap in the mean equity share is still 0.57-0.72 at 64x48.
   4. This is the early-life rent-obligation problem of `TODO.md` item 1
      (rent is 24% of income at 25), not old-age bankruptcy. No placement
      fixes it.

## 4. What the converged model says

The converged answer is a corner. Every owner aged 25-39 consumes at the
solver's lower bound, `c_floor_frac` = 1% of total wealth (about EUR 3-5k a
year). Liquid savings reach EUR 3.2M at 80 and consumption peaks at EUR 275k at
90. Households hoard against old-age bankruptcy from maintenance on an
appreciating, unsellable house. The coarse grid hid this. The bound is a
numerical device acting as an economic constraint, so at step 7 the young
owners' behaviour is not an interior choice. A better grid shows the problem;
it does not fix it. The fix is in the economics (section 5, item 2).

## 5. Decisions open, in order

1. **Grid placement for housing steps.** Options:
   1. Power 2 on both axes (`grid_pow = 2`, `grid_pow_u2 = 2`) at about 64x48.
      Both are existing primitives. It is the closest to the reference, at
      about 10 minutes per tenure per step.
   2. The log-wealth rule. It is better at 32x32 but needs a new primitive to
      select it.
   3. Keep the current grid and report the step as not converged.

   Changing a grid primitive in `config.params` makes every saved result
   stale, so the ladder re-solves. For the renter no placement converges in
   early life (section 3, item 5). Before adopting a placement, check it on
   steps 1-6 and 9-12. On 13 October, bunching u1 low thinned the points
   young households use in the no-housing steps.
2. **The owner's housing economics** (`TODO.md` item 1). The choices are
   equity release or partial sale when cash runs short, a mortgage payment
   tied to the loan or to income rather than the current house value, what
   the house is for (bequest `chi > 0`), a sourced floor (bijstand), and the
   renter's rent cap. Whether the step-7 corner is a result or an artefact
   depends on this.
3. **`c_floor_frac`.** In the converged owner solve it binds for 100% of
   household-years at 25-39, 99% at 40-66 and 48% from 67 (renters: 78% at
   25-39). It is 1% of total wealth including the house, capped at half of
   liquid resources. Decide it together with the floor.
   `docs/SOLVER_BOUNDS.md` documents it and the solver's other numerical
   bounds.
4. **Steps 9-12.** Rerun on a finer or bunched grid before quoting sizes
   around retirement.
5. **Still open from part 1** (`TODO.md` item 2): the entry buffer's
   denominator, `tau_inc` against DHS net over gross, and the housing level
   placeholders.

## 6. How to reproduce, in order

From the repository root in MATLAB. Inside `matlab -batch` from a bash shell,
pass name lists as `string({'a', 'b'})`, not `["a", "b"]`, because the double
quotes do not survive the Windows command line.

1. `ladder.run(7)` and `ladder.robustness(7)` at standard numerics.
   `ruin_figures` needs the step-7 results and the doubled-income-axis solve.
2. `addpath diagnostics/step07; solve_cases`: the step-7 variants, including
   constant house prices (about 25 minutes).
3. `ruin_figures`: figures 1-5 for step 7 as run, constant house prices as the
   contrast. Each figure's text is also written to a `.txt` file next to it.
4. `addpath diagnostics/grid_design; solve_grids`: the default cases
   (log-wealth 16x16, 32x32, 64x48; current 32x32, 64x48).
5. `solve_grids(cases=string({'edge_16x16', 'edge_32x32', 'logwealth_128x96', 'power_16x16', 'power3_16x16', 'power_32x32', 'power3_32x32', 'power_64x48'}))`.
   About an hour, half of it the 128x96 solve.
6. `compare_grids(ref='logwealth_128x96')`, and with `owner=false` for renters (run
   `solve_grids(owner=false, ...)` first): the table above, the robustness
   page and the overlay.
7. Figures 1-3 for any pair:
   `ruin_figures([], files={coarse, fine, contrast_coarse, contrast_fine}, names={...}, main='...', alt='...', tag='_x', out=..., figures=1:3)`.
8. The mortgage chart:
   `figures.mortgage(r, file, panels='payment', nominal=false)`.
9. Twelve-panel dashboards: `figures.dashboard(r, file)`.

## 8. How Quinn wants results shown

1. **Figures, not tables.** A twelve-panel dashboard per solve, a comparison
   showing both 10-90 bands, and a robustness page with the reference in
   black and levels beside the differences.
2. **Income in the consumption panel.** Every comparison page draws mean gross
   income and income after tax, contributions and housing behind consumption.
   This is now built into `figures.compare` and `figures.robustness`.
3. **Units in words.** Write "years of household income", not "x income".
   Value axes are labelled "value function", with ticks showing the
   certainty-equivalent consumption and, in brackets, V in units where a year
   of consuming one's income is -1.
4. **Axes.** Grid graphs put house value across and savings up, both linear,
   with one range shared by all panels.
5. **Titles.** The figure titles of figures 1-3 are Quinn's wording; keep
   them.
6. **The floor stays at 1e-6.** Quinn does not want other floors tested.
