## 2. What was found

### 2.1 The pension steps were not converged, and the pension is the cause

1. On the ladder's standard grid (evenly spaced 16x16) every DC step is off
   around retirement: liquid saving at 60 is 16-37% too high (about double at
   66 at steps 10-11) and consumption at 66 is 3-11% too high. Step 6, without
   the pension, converges on the same grid. Directions between steps survive;
   sizes around retirement do not.
2. Why: with a pot, retirees hold 15-60 years of income in total resources and
   little liquid saving beside the pot (low u1, u2 near 1). The evenly spaced
   axes have no point between 14.8 and 1,250 years of income and two points
   above u2 = 0.93.
3. A storage artefact on top. Where nothing is saved the solver stored an
   equity share of 1 (the rule of 15 October), and savers next to that corner
   interpolated it. At step 10 the savers' share at 66 came out at 0.37-0.45
   instead of about 0.01. The primitive `pi_nothing_saved = 'limit'` stores the
   share of a vanishing saving instead; the value is the same either way.
4. The fix, numerical only: points bunched toward low u1 and toward u2 = 1
   (`grid_pow = 3`, `grid_pow_u2 = 3`) with the limit share. Refinement is then
   monotone; 32x32 is within about 1% in consumption, 48x48 within the stop
   rule at every DC step. Once both axes are bunched, extra points pay most on
   u1 (64x32 about as good as 48x48). Quadrature at 7 nodes instead of 5 moves
   consumption by at most 0.2%.
5. Points that follow age (`grid_nodes.u1_age`, `u2_age`, opt-in): better per
   point in working life, but late-retirement savings run down too fast. Not
   ready.

### 2.2 The same fix works with housing

1. Step 7b (house, no pension), maintenance on the purchase price: the owner
   is within the stop rule of bunched 64x64 already at bunched 16x16. The
   ladder's evenly spaced 16x16 is 5.1% off in retirement consumption and 0.13
   in the retirement equity share. A grid evenly spaced in log wealth gives the
   same answer when fine, but is worse at 16x16 (2.6% against 0.3% in
   retirement).
2. Step 13 (house and pension), same settings: the owner's 24x24 is within the
   stop rule of 32x32 at every age (consumption within 0.8%, the equity share
   of liquid saving within 0.013, liquid savings within 0.11 years of income).
   16x16 just misses: 2.3% in consumption at 66, 0.06 in the share at 55-62.
   The entry value settles: 0.815, 0.807, 0.802 at 16, 24, 32.
3. So owners need fewer points at step 13 than at the DC steps without housing
   (48x48), and more than at step 7b (16x16). One rule, power 3 on both wealth
   axes with the limit share, works throughout.

### 2.3 The u3 axis at step 13

1. With the house and the pension together, u3 = A/(A+H), the pot against pot
   plus house, is a live axis. Its 10 evenly spaced points are enough except
   for the equity share of owners past about 88: doubling u3 moves it by 0.02
   at 88, 0.08 at 93 and 0.14 at 95. Their pot has fallen below a tenth of pot
   plus house, below the first point above zero (0.11).
2. New option `grid_pow_u3` (default 1, evenly spaced). At 2 the same 10 points
   bunch toward zero (0, 0.012, 0.049, 0.111, ...). Against u3 doubled the
   owner's share is then within 0.010 up to 92 and 0.024 at 95; at 95 u3
   doubled is itself the coarser grid near zero. The cost: owners' liquid
   savings at 40-80 move 2-5% against u3 doubled (1-4% evenly spaced), within
   the stop rule. Same solve time.
3. Renters are not worse off with power 2, and closer in retirement.
4. Not run: power 2 at 20 points, which would show whether both ends agree.

### 2.4 The grid search before fmincon

1. At step 13 the global (c, pi) search barely changes the answer. Without it
   the renter is within the stop rule at 16x16 and 32x32, and the owner at
   16x16 except the mean share of young owners who save almost nothing (0.35
   off at 27; weighted by saving, 0.03). The owner's entry value is slightly
   lower without it (0.812 against 0.815). It halves the solve time.
2. At the DC steps on 12 October it mattered (the share 0.28-0.43 off at 26-28
   and up to 1.0 at 95). Whether to keep it is a choice per step.

### 2.5 The renter does not converge before 40, at any step, on any grid

1. On each finer grid young renters consume less and save more. At step 13 the
   entry value falls from 0.60 at 16x16 to 0.54, 0.47 and 0.27 at 24, 32 and
   48. The same at step 7b.
2. From 40 on the renter converges slowly: consumption within 2% at 32x32 (1.7%
   and 0.7% against 48x48 at 40-66 and 67-95), the equity share 0.05 off at
   40-66.
3. The cause is the model, not the numerics: rent is 24% of gross income at
   entry (`alpha * h_mult` = 0.06 x 4), about 42% of net, it cannot be reduced,
   and it grows 0.99% a year in real terms (CBS regulated rents) while
   retirement income is flat. The only protection is the floor of 1e-6 times
   income with the shortfall written off; at gamma = 5 one year at that floor
   weighs as much as 1e24 normal years, so it is a ruin penalty, not a safety
   net. That is TODO item 1.

### 2.6 Run times and speed-ups

1. Two exact speed-ups copy grid points no household can reach instead of
   solving them: without housing, u3 = 0 from u3 = 1; without a DC pillar,
   u3 = 1 from u3 = 0. Identical answers, about half the solve time.
2. Step 13, bunched, two MATLAB processes at a time (alone about a third
   shorter): owner 70, 151 and 236 minutes at 16x16, 24x24 and 32x32; renter 7,
   14, 24 and 53 minutes at 16x16 to 48x48. Doubling u3 doubles the time;
   dropping the grid search halves it.
3. On this 16-core PC two processes give about 1.4 times the throughput of
   one; more than two do not help.

## 3. Decisions open, in order

1. The floor and the rent burden (TODO.md, item 1). It decides whether anything
   before 40 can be reported, for renters at every housing step: a floor at the
   social minimum (bijstand), housing benefit (huurtoeslag) or downsizing for
   renters, equity release for owners.
2. The ladder's grid. `ladder.numerics` sets only `grid_dims` and `gh_n`.
   Carrying the placement (`grid_pow` 3, `grid_pow_u2` 3, and `grid_pow_u3` 2
   where u3 is live) is a code change, and every saved ladder result would
   re-solve. Sizes found: 48x48 at the DC steps, 24x24 for owners at step 13,
   16x16 at step 7b.
3. The default of `pi_nothing_saved`: 'limit' is recommended (HANDOFF.md, 16
   October); it differs from 'one' only where the first euro saved would not go
   to stocks.
4. The default of `grid_pow_u3`, and whether to run power 2 at 20 points first.
5. Whether the ladder keeps the grid search (`use_refine`) at every step, or
   drops it where it does not change the answer (step 13).
6. Points that follow age: keep as an option or remove.

## 5. How to reproduce, in order

From the repo root in MATLAB:

1. `addpath diagnostics/pension_grid diagnostics/grid_design;
   setenv('CGM_OUTPUT_DIR', pwd)`.
2. Solve cases with `solve_cases(["13:owner:pow3_24x24_lim_mop", ...])`. A case
   is `<step>:<arm>:<rule>_<N1>x<N2>` plus suffixes (`case_params.m`): `_lim`
   limit share, `_mop` maintenance on the purchase price, `_u3x2` u3 doubled,
   `_u3p<n>` u3 at power n, `_nosweep` no grid search, `_gh<n>` quadrature.
   Rules: `uniform`, `pow3`, `logw` and others listed there. Results go to
   `ladder/standard_16x16x10x8_gh5/pension_grid/NN_<step>_<arm>_<case>.mat`;
   a case already saved for the same calibration is skipped.
3. Long runs: two `matlab -batch` processes in the background, each with a list
   of cases, smallest grids first.
4. Gaps: `case_gaps(case_load(13, 'owner', 'pow3_16x16_lim_mop'),
   case_load(13, 'owner', 'pow3_32x32_lim_mop'))`, by age band 26-39, 40-66,
   67-95.
5. Pages: `report(cover_lines)` (the DC study), `essence_pdf()` (its five-page
   summary), `housing_pdf(cover_lines, '7b')` and `housing_pdf(cover_lines, 13)`.
