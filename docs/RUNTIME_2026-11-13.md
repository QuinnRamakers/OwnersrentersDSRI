# Owner runtime: what was measured and what was changed (12-13 November 2026)

A summary of the work logged in `HANDOFF.md` on 12 and 13 November. The
numbers come from the DSRI pod (32 threads) and from the saved owner solves of
step 19, b = 2.5, on `pow3_32x8_lim_u3n40_qn4_as_nc11` (34x10x40x5 nodes).
The saved solves used the calibration before 9 November.

## The question

Owner solves had grown to 2.4 hours at 5 quadrature nodes and 4.9 hours with
10 q points, and production's grid is larger still. The aim: make the solve
faster without changing the model or, in any way that matters, the results.

## How it was measured

A saved solve can be replayed one age at a time: the value function and the
policies of age t+1 go in, age t comes out. Run with the repo's own step, the
replay reproduces the saved solve in every bit. That gives a cheap test for
any change to the solver: replay a sample of nodes at a few ages and compare.
The scripts (`runs/perf_probe*.m`, `runs/pow_check/`, not committed) write
timed copies of `+solver/bellman_step_lna.m` and run them.

## Where the time went

1. The global sweep over (c, pi) took 87% and fmincon 13%, nearly all of it
   objective calls. fmincon's own overhead is under 1%.
2. Inside both, 58% was the two powers x^(1-gamma), of the continuation value
   and of the bequest, and 33% the 4-D interpolation.
3. MATLAB's `x.^(-4)` costs about 50 ns a number; `1./(x.*x).^2` costs 0.6 ns.
4. From 67, with production's bond fund, a period reads 125 distinct quadrature
   nodes of the 625.
5. On the pod, 32 threads are about 20 times faster than one. The CPU quota
   throttles the pod in three quarters of the 100 ms periods at 32 threads.
6. MEX functions do not run on thread workers. A process pool of 4 works.

## What was changed: `fast_power`

One arithmetic operation. With gamma = 5 the objective raises numbers to the
power -4 at every quadrature node of every candidate choice. The code now does
this by multiplication (square, square, one over) instead of MATLAB's general
power. The model, the grid, the quadrature, the sweep and fmincon are as
before.

1. It is a numerics switch in `config.params`, `fast_power`, on by default.
   Off, the code is the old code in every bit. The case suffix `_mpow` sets it
   off.
2. It applies when 1 - gamma is a whole number from -1 to -32, so also at
   gamma = 10 (ninth power). With any other gamma MATLAB's power is used.
   No solve has been run with it at gamma = 10.
3. The two routes differ in the 16th digit. At nodes where two choices are
   worth almost the same, that can tip the search to the other choice.

Results against the saved solves:

| | Before | After | Faster by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner, 5 nodes | 8,803 s | 4,379 s | 2.01 |
| Owner, 3 nodes (same day) | 1,489 s | 849 s | 1.75 |
| Renter, 3 nodes | 242 s | 136 s | 1.78 |

Largest gap at any age in the simulated panel (same households and shocks):

| | Owner, 5 nodes | Owner, 3 nodes | Renter, 3 nodes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean consumption | 0.0007% | 0.0021% | 0.0032% |
| Mean liquid saving | 0.0017% | 0.0053% | 0.0018% |
| Liquid equity share | 0.0037 | 0.0011 | 0.0001 |
| Share at the floor, entry value | none | none | none |

At single grid nodes the certainty equivalent differs by more than 1e-4 at
0.6% (5 nodes) and 0.9% (3 nodes) of owner node-ages, and under 0.001% of the
renters'. `model.checks` are as saved in all three.

After the change the interpolation is 77% of the sweep. A bilinear lookup
written by hand in MATLAB is no faster than `griddedInterpolant`.

## What was measured and not changed

1. Evaluating equal quadrature nodes once. Identical in every bit, and 3.2 to
   3.8 times faster at retired ages, but only when the fund holds no REIT
   after 67. With stocks and the REIT in the fund all 625 nodes differ.
2. Retired ages on three draws (stock, house, REIT) instead of four. After 66
   income has no shock. The correlations between the three are the same to 12
   digits; the quadrature points are not (125 for 625), because the code
   builds the stock, house and REIT values out of the income draw. A retired
   age becomes 3.4 to 4 times faster. With the income correlations of
   9 November the median node moves by 2e-5 to 5e-5 in the certainty
   equivalent and 0.01 to 0.02 in the equity share. The order of the three
   draws matters as much: with the stock draw last the 125 points lie closest
   to a fine reference. See `docs/prompts/cholesky_order.md`.
3. A coarser first round of the sweep (11 x 11). 1.7 times faster and not
   safe: the value is lower by more than 1e-4 at about 4% of nodes.

## Findings on the side

1. The repo's search misses a better point, by more than 1e-4 in value, at
   about 1% of nodes.
2. With the income correlations of 9 November the retired ages depend on
   those correlations, through the quadrature points, although retirees have
   no income risk.
3. The test `bequest_off_identical` fails with or without `fast_power`: its
   step-18 baseline of 26 October predates the pooled income of 29 October.
4. The paper has default as a choice (the larger of two values). The code
   triggers it when liquid resources fall below the floor.
